    I talk to a lot of Olim about finding work and it’s usually the same two worries - Israel is expensive and how can I land a job? both fair But there’s another route that most Olim never consider: starting a business  True, it’s not always the most straightforward path and it comes with unique challenges - bureaucratic, cultural and otherwise. But it is a path. For some people the way to land a job is actually to employ yourself Doing all this is easier said than done of course From building up a customer base in Israel to haggling with vendors and suppliers, to even just dealing with the quirky minutiae of opening a business bank account, choosing a VAT taxation track, paying into your own Pension and saving funds (Hishtalmut) and hiring employees On Sunday Nathan and I had Barak Hadad on Next Million to give us the full low down He made Aliyah from Belgium 5 months ago and single-handedly set up Par 4 Golf Israel in Herzliya Pituach - an indoor golf simulator he started building before he’d even moved For him that meant speaking to 4 banks (Mizrahi Tefahot was the one that finally listened), getting 2 AC quotes 160k shek apart, dealing with the Iran war in the middle of the build-out and not being able to secure a car lease until he had 3 months of Israeli payslips, or as he put it, “everything takes time in Israel” He found out that the first quote is never the final price, never take at face value what people promise over the phone - get everything in writing and sometimes the Sales reps will call you at 10pm until you sign with them and then you’ll never hear from them again He left Belgium and a career in Finance for this because - he loves golf, he saw a unique business need (Israel has only 1 golf course), he wanted to raise his kids in Israel and of course, who wouldn’t want to go play some golf in Herzliya? One of his main tips was to work out what an “OK” life here costs per month and then build your business plan based on that number And most of the good people you’ll work with come through someone you already know, so networking is key in SmBs as well...more

    Your phone, your laptop and nearly every digital device you own exist because one Israeli engineer asked a question no one else thought to ask. In the 1980s, computers were advancing fast, but storage was slow, fragile and expensive. Systems depended on bulky magnetic disks that failed easily and couldn’t handle the growing demands of a digital world. At Intel’s Haifa lab, engineer Dov Frohman began searching for something better. He wondered whether a chip could store information even without power, in a compact and durable form. The idea was radical at the time. Many experts dismissed it as impossible. But in 1980, Frohman and his team achieved a breakthrough: electrically erasable programmable read-only memory, or EEPROM. This innovation became the foundation for NAND and NOR flash memory. Flash storage revolutionized everything. It made smartphones, USB drives, digital cameras, solid-state drives and modern computing possible. It made technology smaller, faster and far more reliable. Entire industries rose from a concept once considered unrealistic. Frohman’s invention turned Israel into a global semiconductor powerhouse and changed the way billions of people store, use and share information. Every text message, every photo, every saved file carries a trace of his legacy. Via @Israel on X
